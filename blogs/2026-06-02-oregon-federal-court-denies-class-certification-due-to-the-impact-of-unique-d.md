A judge rejected class certification when unique defenses affecting named plaintiffs' claims differed materially from those applicable to potential class members. The ruling reinforces the principle that named plaintiffs must be typical representatives of the proposed class without individualized vulnerabilities. This decision is useful precedent for defendants seeking to oppose class certification where named plaintiffs face distinctive legal exposure.
